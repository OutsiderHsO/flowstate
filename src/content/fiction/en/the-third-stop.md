## I

One lamp on the platform was broken. Not dark — the flashing kind, lighting a patch of standing water every few seconds and then taking it back.

He pulled his hood down. Wind came out of the tunnel carrying rust and rain.

"Third stop," she said.

Only then did he notice someone else standing there. The broken lamp stretched her shadow long enough to spill over the platform edge and hang above the rails.

## II

The last train had just left. The next would not be along for forty minutes.

"You waited for the wrong one too?" he asked.

"I didn't wait for the wrong one." She pushed her hands into her pockets. "I just didn't want to go back this early."

He did not answer that. Some sentences do not want an answer; taking them up only sounds like an interrogation.

---

The rain thinned. The lamp kept flashing, its rhythm unchanged, like something refusing to stop.

## III

Forty minutes later the train came.

She boarded first and stayed by the door without going in. He followed and stopped two steps behind her.

The carriage was empty enough that he could hear the wheels crossing every joint. The broken lamp stayed behind on the platform, flashing, shrinking, until it was a single point and then gone.

She turned and said something. The wind was too loud. He did not catch it, and did not want to ask twice.
