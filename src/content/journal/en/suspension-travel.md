## The premise

Low looks good. Nobody argues with that. The argument is about who pays for it.

In a typical "drop it 30mm" job, the thing that actually changes is **usable travel**. Less travel means every speed bump, expansion joint and unpatched trench is caught by a stiffer spring and a harder damper.

## Reading the numbers

A fully fictional comparison table, used here to show the table styles:

| Parameter | Stock | Sample change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Static ground clearance | 140 mm | 110 mm |
| Available compression | 78 mm | 51 mm |
| Spring rate increase | — | +22% |
| Rebound damping increase | — | +15% |

What matters is not one absolute value but the **ratio** between compression travel and damping. Stiffness up thirty percent with damping up fifteen typically means the body keeps moving after each bump — every rebound overshoots a little.

## The bill

- **Comfort**: more high-frequency buzz, fatigue arrives earlier on long drives.
- **Durability**: damper temperatures climb, seal life shortens.
- **Access**: underground car park ramps become objects of study.

## One line

Stance is a **mortgage**: you borrow the look against your margin for error. Whether it is worth it depends on whether you drive *that* road, or only *those* roads.
